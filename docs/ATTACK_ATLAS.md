**42 curated vectors** across 8 families, 7 payment rails and 6 attack surfaces, plus **10 machine-composed hybrids**. 14 have a working generator in this repository.

Scope note: entries describe observable behaviour and telemetry signatures — what a defender can model. They deliberately contain no operational playbooks, tooling or infrastructure. See `docs/RESPONSIBLE_USE.md`.


## Threat scoring

```
0.30 x detection_gap + 0.24 x genai_uplift + 0.20 x impact
  + 0.15 x scale_velocity + 0.11 x feasibility
```

Weighted so that *what we cannot currently see* dominates: a cheap, scalable attack the incumbent stack misses deserves more red-team attention than an expensive one it already catches.

## Simulated vectors, in detail


### AV-ADV-PERTURB — Adversarial feature perturbation under realism constraints

**Family** Attacks on the defence · **Surface** model · **Rails** card_cnp, upi_p2p, wallet · **Threat** 97.8 (critical)

Attack parameters that the attacker fully controls — amount, timing, merchant selection, device hygiene, beneficiary count, session pacing — are optimised to minimise the model's score while keeping the attack profitable.

**Why GenAI changes it.** This is the meta-vector, and it is exactly what the IMMUNIS red agent implements. Any attacker with score feedback can run the same optimisation, which is precisely why the defence must run it first.

**Kill chain**

1. Controllable attack parameters enumerated
2. Score or outcome feedback obtained from the target
3. Parameters optimised for minimum score subject to a value floor
4. Optimised strain deployed at scale until the model is retrained

**Observable signals**

- population of attacks clustering just below the operating threshold
- score distribution of confirmed fraud shifting downward over time
- attack parameters converging across otherwise unrelated incidents
- detection recall decaying while transaction distributions are stable

**Mitigations**

- Continuous adversarial self-play — retrain on your own evasions
- Ensemble and rule diversity so no single boundary can be optimised against
- Monitor the score distribution of confirmed fraud as a drift alarm

**Historical analogue** Adversarial examples in vision and malware classifiers  
**Who it hits** Every model in the network simultaneously  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `__redteam__`
  
**Note** Implemented as the red agent in redteam/evader.py rather than as a standalone injector.


### AV-DIGITAL-ARREST — Coercion-authorised payment ("digital arrest")

**Family** Social engineering / APP · **Surface** authorisation · **Rails** upi_p2p, remittance · **Threat** 94.8 (critical)

Victim is held in a sustained impersonated-authority video/voice call and coerced into transferring funds to a 'verification' account. Generative video and voice make the impersonation persuasive; an LLM sustains a multi-hour interrogation script that adapts to the victim's objections.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Pre-GenAI this required a fluent, confident human operator per victim and collapsed under improvised questions. An LLM runs the interrogation indefinitely, in the victim's own language and register, at ~zero marginal cost per victim, while synthetic video supplies the uniform and the office.

**Kill chain**

1. Target selection from breached or scraped personal data
2. Inbound contact impersonating law enforcement or a telecom regulator
3. Sustained isolation: victim kept on a live call, told not to contact anyone
4. Escalation to a 'verification transfer' framed as temporary and reversible
5. Victim authorises the payment themselves on their own device
6. Immediate layering through a mule chain within minutes of receipt

**Observable signals**

- unusually long session with screen-share or call active during payment
- first-ever transfer to this beneficiary, created minutes before the payment
- transfer amount far above the customer's own historical p95
- abnormal hesitation and retry pattern during entry (coerced, not habitual)
- beneficiary account age measured in days with high fan-in
- sequence of escalating transfers within a single session window
- payment initiated outside the customer's normal circadian window

**Mitigations**

- Cross-modal detection: fuse in-session context (call/screen-share state) with the transaction
- Cooling-off and named-beneficiary confirmation for first-time high-value payees
- Beneficiary-side velocity and account-age gating at the receiving bank

**Historical analogue** Courier/CBI impersonation scams; IRS callback fraud  
**Who it hits** Senior citizens, first-generation digital users, students abroad  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `digital_arrest`


### AV-AGENT-INJECT — Prompt-injected shopping agent

**Family** Agentic commerce · **Surface** authorisation · **Rails** agentic, card_cnp · **Threat** 93.8 (critical)

An autonomous shopping agent acting for a consumer encounters attacker-controlled content on a merchant page that redirects its purchasing goal — different item, different merchant, different amount — while the consumer's credential and mandate remain entirely genuine.

**Why GenAI changes it.** This vector does not exist without generative AI. The agent is the victim, the payment credential is valid, and no existing fraud model has ever seen a labelled example.

**Kill chain**

1. Consumer delegates a purchase to an autonomous agent
2. Agent traverses attacker-influenced merchant content
3. Agent's objective is redirected by injected instructions
4. Purchase completed at an attacker-benefiting merchant under a valid mandate
5. Consumer discovers the divergence only at reconciliation

**Observable signals**

- purchase category divergent from the stated mandate intent
- agent session traversing an unusually long or unusual merchant path
- merchant novel to both the consumer and the agent platform
- amount at the mandate ceiling rather than at a natural price point
- time-to-decision far shorter than the agent's normal comparison behaviour

**Mitigations**

- Mandate-intent conformance checking at authorisation
- Agent-path attestation and merchant allow-listing
- Treat the mandate as a constraint the network enforces, not advice

**Historical analogue** Malvertising redirects; supply-chain content injection  
**Who it hits** Early adopters of agentic checkout  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `agent_inject`


### AV-AGENT-MANDATE — Over-scoped or manipulated agent payment mandate

**Family** Agentic commerce · **Surface** authorisation · **Rails** agentic, wallet · **Threat** 90.8 (critical)

The consumer is induced to grant an agent a payment mandate far broader than they understand — higher ceiling, longer validity, wider merchant scope — which is then drawn down in full.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Consent interfaces for agent mandates are new, inconsistent and poorly understood; persuasive generated framing does the rest.

**Kill chain**

1. Consumer onboards an agent with an over-broad mandate
2. Mandate scope obscured by persuasive consent framing
3. Drawdown begins within the technically-authorised envelope
4. Full ceiling consumed across merchants before the consumer notices

**Observable signals**

- mandate ceiling far above the consumer's own historical spend distribution
- drawdown rate approaching the ceiling within the first mandate period
- merchant scope breadth inconsistent with the stated purpose
- no human confirmation events anywhere in the mandate's lifetime
- mandate created and first used within an unusually short window

**Mitigations**

- Network-enforced mandate ceilings benchmarked against the consumer's history
- Progressive trust: small initial ceilings that grow with observed behaviour
- Mandatory human confirmation above a spend-distribution percentile

**Historical analogue** Over-permissive OAuth scopes; recurring-mandate abuse  
**Who it hits** Consumers adopting agent-initiated payments  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `agent_mandate`
  
**Note** Held out of training in the zero-day evaluation.


### AV-SYNTH-ID — Synthetic identity manufacture and credit bust-out

**Family** Identity / onboarding · **Surface** onboarding · **Rails** card_cnp, card_cp, wallet · **Threat** 87.8 (critical)

A fabricated identity — real-looking but belonging to nobody — is nurtured with genuine small transactions and prompt repayment for months, until credit limits are large enough to draw down entirely and abandon.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Generative models produce coherent identity backstories, supporting documents and, critically, *plausible transaction behaviour* during the nurture phase, so the account looks like a real thin-file customer.

**Kill chain**

1. Identity assembled from partially real and partially fabricated attributes
2. Onboarding at a thin-file-friendly institution
3. Months of small, well-behaved, promptly-repaid activity
4. Credit limits raised on demonstrated good behaviour
5. Coordinated maximum drawdown across all lines, then abandonment

**Observable signals**

- thin identity footprint: no long-lived cross-institution history
- behaviour statistically *too* well-behaved during nurture
- utilisation step-change from low and stable to near-limit within days
- shared device, address or contact attributes across many 'unrelated' identities
- cash-equivalent and high-liquidity MCC concentration at bust-out

**Mitigations**

- Cross-institution identity-footprint scoring at onboarding
- Monitor utilisation *acceleration*, not just level
- Entity resolution across device/address/contact graphs

**Historical analogue** Classic bust-out fraud; Frankenstein identities  
**Who it hits** Issuers and lenders rather than consumers — no victim disputes it  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `synth_id`


### AV-DEEPFAKE-KYC — Deepfake and injection attacks on video KYC

**Family** Identity / onboarding · **Surface** onboarding · **Rails** upi_p2p, wallet, card_cnp · **Threat** 87.8 (critical)

Remote identity verification is defeated either by presenting a synthetic face to the camera or by injecting a synthetic video stream directly into the capture pipeline, bypassing the camera altogether.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Face swapping now runs in real time on commodity hardware and passes most active-liveness challenges; injection attacks skip the physical channel that liveness checks assume exists.

**Kill chain**

1. Identity documents assembled or forged
2. Synthetic face rendered in real time to match the document
3. Liveness challenge satisfied, or the capture pipeline bypassed entirely
4. Account opened and immediately used as a receiving/mule account

**Observable signals**

- capture-pipeline integrity anomalies (virtual camera, frame timing regularity)
- account moves to high fan-in receiving behaviour within days of opening
- onboarding device shared with previously flagged accounts
- document and selfie both novel with no historical footprint

**Mitigations**

- Injection-attack detection at the capture layer, not just liveness
- Post-onboarding behavioural gating for the first 30 days
- Device and network reuse detection across onboarding events

**Historical analogue** Photo-of-a-photo presentation attacks  
**Who it hits** Institutions and, downstream, every victim paying into the mule  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `deepfake_kyc`


### AV-FAKE-MERCH — Synthetic merchant onboarding for transaction laundering

**Family** Merchant / acquiring · **Surface** settlement · **Rails** card_cnp, upi_p2m · **Threat** 87.8 (critical)

A fully-generated business — website, catalogue, reviews, incorporation documents, social presence — is onboarded by an acquirer and used to process transactions for goods that do not exist or are not permitted.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Merchant due diligence leans heavily on 'does this business look real'. Generating an entire plausible commercial footprint is now hours of work.

**Kill chain**

1. Complete synthetic business footprint generated
2. Acquirer onboarding passed on documentary and web presence checks
3. Genuine-looking low-value volume processed to build history
4. Volume ramped sharply, then settlement drawn and entity abandoned

**Observable signals**

- web and business footprint created entirely within a short window
- transaction mix statistically unlike the declared MCC's population
- customer base with no repeat purchasers
- sharp volume ramp shortly after reserve release
- refund and chargeback profile inconsistent with the stated business

**Mitigations**

- Behavioural merchant scoring post-onboarding, not just KYB at onboarding
- MCC-population conformance testing on transaction mix
- Dynamic reserve policy tied to volume acceleration

**Historical analogue** Bust-out merchants; transaction laundering  
**Who it hits** Acquirers, who carry the settlement exposure  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `fake_merchant`


### AV-VOICE-CLONE — Cloned-voice relative-in-distress / executive request

**Family** Social engineering / APP · **Surface** authorisation · **Rails** upi_p2p, card_cnp, remittance · **Threat** 87.0 (critical)

A few seconds of public audio is enough to clone a voice. The victim receives a call from a familiar voice requesting an urgent transfer, typically framed as an accident, arrest, or a confidential deal.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Voice cloning moved from studio-grade to consumer-grade; the constraint is no longer the audio, it is the target list.

**Kill chain**

1. Voice sample harvested from public media
2. Relationship graph inferred from social profiles
3. Urgent inbound call with a plausible, verifiable-sounding pretext
4. Payment requested to a 'friend's' account to avoid name mismatch checks
5. Rapid dispersal after receipt

**Observable signals**

- new beneficiary with a name unrelated to any prior payee
- amount at or just under a step-up threshold
- compressed decision time between beneficiary creation and payment
- device and geo normal — only the payee and amount are anomalous

**Mitigations**

- Out-of-band verification prompts on first payment to a new payee
- Payee-name-matching (confirmation of payee) at the network layer
- Family-designated 'safe word' controls in the issuer app

**Historical analogue** Grandparent scam; classic BEC wire request  
**Who it hits** Parents of students abroad, finance staff in SMEs  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `voice_clone`


### AV-MULE-LAYER — Fan-in / fan-out mule layering

**Family** Money movement · **Surface** settlement · **Rails** upi_p2p, wallet · **Threat** 87.0 (critical)

Proceeds are received by a first-layer mule and dispersed within minutes across a widening tree of accounts, with hop counts, amounts and timing chosen to stay under every per-account reporting and velocity threshold.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Route and split optimisation against known thresholds is an automated planning problem; the structures produced are far less regular — and therefore far harder to rule-match — than hand-built ones.

**Kill chain**

1. Proceeds land in a first-layer receiving account
2. Split into multiple onward transfers within minutes
3. Successive layers widen the tree and cross institutions
4. Terminal accounts cash out via ATM, merchant purchase or off-ramp

**Observable signals**

- pass-through ratio near 1.0 with dwell time in minutes
- in-degree and out-degree spiking simultaneously on a young account
- amount splits summing to the received value minus a small retained fee
- graph community with high internal density and no external economic activity
- balance never held overnight

**Mitigations**

- Graph features (fan-in/out, pass-through ratio, community risk) in real time
- Cross-institution beneficiary intelligence at the network layer
- Hold periods on first-time high-value credits to young accounts

**Historical analogue** Smurfing; layering  
**Who it hits** Upstream fraud victims; institutions carry AML exposure  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `mule_layer`


### AV-FRIENDLY-FRAUD — Generated dispute narratives (first-party misuse)

**Family** Merchant / acquiring · **Surface** dispute · **Rails** card_cnp, card_cp · **Threat** 86.0 (critical)

Cardholders dispute genuine transactions using generated narratives tuned to the exact chargeback reason code most likely to succeed, industrialising what used to be an occasional, clumsy consumer behaviour.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Reason-code selection and narrative construction used to require expertise. It is now a prompt, and the resulting text is more coherent than most genuine disputes.

**Kill chain**

1. Genuine purchase completed and goods received
2. Reason code selected for maximum success probability
3. Dispute narrative generated to match that code's evidentiary standard
4. Chargeback pursued; merchant representment often abandoned on cost

**Observable signals**

- dispute rate per cardholder far above portfolio norms
- disputed transactions carrying strong device and behavioural continuity
- narrative similarity across a cardholder's disputes and across the portfolio
- delivery, session and geolocation evidence contradicting the claim

**Mitigations**

- Attach device/session continuity evidence automatically to representments
- Cardholder-level dispute-propensity scoring
- Narrative-similarity clustering at the network level

**Historical analogue** Friendly fraud; chargeback abuse  
**Who it hits** Merchants primarily; issuers through dispute cost  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `friendly_fraud`


### AV-BIO-CLONE — Behavioural-biometric cloning

**Family** Authentication · **Surface** authentication · **Rails** card_cnp, upi_p2m, wallet · **Threat** 85.6 (critical)

Passive behavioural authentication — typing cadence, swipe dynamics, navigation rhythm, hold pressure — is defeated by a model trained on captured interaction telemetry to reproduce the victim's motor signature.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Behavioural biometrics were considered un-forgeable because they are involuntary. Sequence models learn them from a modest telemetry sample, turning the most trusted silent control into a false negative generator.

**Kill chain**

1. Interaction telemetry captured from a compromised session or app clone
2. Motor-signature model fitted to the victim's cadence
3. Replay of synthesised interaction during an attacker-driven session
4. Behavioural score passes; step-up is never triggered
5. Transaction executes with the silent control fully satisfied

**Observable signals**

- behavioural score suspiciously *close* to the customer's mean — real humans vary more than their model does
- variance collapse in inter-keystroke intervals across a session
- perfect behavioural match combined with novel device or network path
- absence of natural correction events (backspaces, mis-taps, scroll overshoot)

**Mitigations**

- Score the *variance* of the behavioural signal, not only its centre
- Combine behavioural signals with challenge-response, never alone
- Treat an unnaturally perfect behavioural match as itself anomalous

**Historical analogue** Fingerprint spoofing; replay attacks on static biometrics  
**Who it hits** Customers of institutions relying on passive behavioural auth  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `bio_clone`
  
**Note** Held out of training in the zero-day evaluation.


### AV-AITM-OTP — Real-time adversary-in-the-middle OTP relay

**Family** Authentication · **Surface** authentication · **Rails** card_cnp, upi_p2m, wallet · **Threat** 83.0 (critical)

A reverse-proxy phishing surface relays the victim's credentials and one-time code to the genuine site in real time, capturing the resulting authenticated session and defeating OTP entirely.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Generative tooling collapses the cost of building and localising a convincing, brand-accurate relay surface, and of writing the lure copy that drives traffic to it.

**Kill chain**

1. Victim driven to a proxying surface that mirrors the genuine site
2. Credentials and one-time code relayed live to the real service
3. Authenticated session token captured
4. Session used immediately from attacker infrastructure
5. High-value transaction pushed inside the stolen session

**Observable signals**

- session continuity break: device fingerprint changes mid-session
- impossible-travel velocity between authentication and transaction
- OTP latency far below or above the customer's habitual response time
- transaction within seconds of authentication on a new device
- absence of the customer's usual navigation path before checkout

**Mitigations**

- Phishing-resistant authentication (FIDO2/passkeys) with origin binding
- Device-binding continuity checks between auth and authorisation
- Session-anomaly features fed directly into the authorisation model

**Historical analogue** Man-in-the-browser trojans; session hijacking  
**Who it hits** Broad; concentrates on high-balance retail customers  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `aitm_otp`


### AV-BIN-ENUM — Distributed BIN enumeration with humanised cadence

**Family** Card / rail exploitation · **Surface** authorisation · **Rails** card_cnp · **Threat** 75.2 (high)

Card numbers, expiry dates and CVVs are validated by micro-authorisations spread across many low-friction merchants, with request timing, basket composition and browsing behaviour generated to look human.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Velocity rules catch machine cadence. Generated traffic now carries human-plausible inter-arrival times, session paths and basket variety, which is precisely what those rules were keyed on.

**Kill chain**

1. Candidate card numbers derived from a target BIN range
2. Validation attempts distributed across many small merchants
3. Timing and session behaviour humanised to defeat velocity rules
4. Validated cards separated for resale or immediate cash-out

**Observable signals**

- elevated decline-to-approval ratio concentrated in a BIN range
- many distinct PANs sharing a device, fingerprint or network segment
- micro-amount authorisations at merchants with no basket coherence
- the same merchant seeing an unusual PAN-diversity spike

**Mitigations**

- Network-level BIN-range anomaly detection across acquirers
- PAN-diversity-per-device limits at the merchant
- Adaptive step-up on low-value authorisations from unfamiliar devices

**Historical analogue** Card testing; enumeration attacks  
**Who it hits** Issuers, and the small merchants used as testing grounds  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `bin_enum`


### AV-TOKEN-PROV — Token provisioning fraud (push provisioning to attacker wallet)

**Family** Card / rail exploitation · **Surface** authentication · **Rails** wallet, card_cp · **Threat** 75.2 (high)

A stolen card is provisioned into the attacker's mobile wallet by satisfying the issuer's identification-and-verification step, converting a compromised number into a contactless credential with high approval rates.

**Why GenAI changes it.** The verification step is often a call-centre or OTP interaction — exactly the surfaces that synthetic voice and real-time relay now defeat.

**Kill chain**

1. Card credentials obtained from a prior compromise
2. Provisioning requested into an attacker-controlled wallet
3. Identification-and-verification step defeated via OTP relay or voice
4. Token used at high-value, low-friction contactless acceptance

**Observable signals**

- provisioning event followed within minutes by first token use
- token device and cardholder device inconsistent
- provisioning geo distant from the card's transaction history
- spend profile after provisioning bearing no resemblance to card history

**Mitigations**

- Risk-based provisioning decisions with device-history checks
- Cooling-off between provisioning and first high-value token use
- Treat provisioning as a first-class risk event in the model

**Historical analogue** Apple Pay / wallet provisioning fraud waves  
**Who it hits** Cardholders; loss usually lands on the issuer  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `token_prov`


### AV-QR-SWAP — QR tampering and collect-request abuse

**Family** Card / rail exploitation · **Surface** authorisation · **Rails** upi_p2m, upi_p2p · **Threat** 67.4 (medium)

Static merchant QR codes are physically overlaid with an attacker's code, or victims are induced to approve an inbound collect request they believe is an incoming payment.

**Why GenAI changes it.** Generated merchant branding, signage and support scripts make overlays and collect pretexts far more convincing and cheap to produce per location.

**Kill chain**

1. Attacker QR overlaid at a physical merchant, or a collect request issued
2. Payer believes they are paying the merchant or receiving funds
3. Funds land in an attacker-controlled VPA
4. Immediate onward layering

**Observable signals**

- payments to a VPA at a merchant location with no acquiring relationship
- many distinct payers to a young VPA within a tight geographic radius
- collect requests approved by payers with no prior relationship to the payee
- merchant's own settlement volume dropping while footfall is unchanged

**Mitigations**

- Merchant-VPA geospatial consistency checks
- Friction and clearer intent language on inbound collect requests
- Payer-diversity-per-young-VPA monitoring

**Historical analogue** Parking-meter QR fraud; payment-request phishing  
**Who it hits** Consumers at physical merchants; small merchants lose revenue  
**Generator** `engine/immunis/generate/attacks/` → `qr_swap`
